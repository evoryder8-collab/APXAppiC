Avatar report FAQ

What counts toward lifted volume?

Ten reps with 12 kg add 120 kg to your total. FITR MAX adds up the external weight and reps you recorded for completed sets within the report dates. Bodyweight, assistance, band tension and timed work don’t add kilograms to that total. Skipped sets don’t count. An asterisk means some records are incomplete.

How are strength changes compared?

The chart shows the heaviest recorded working load for each session. To compare like with like across two periods, FITR MAX can also compare sets of the same exercise at the same rep count: it takes the heaviest matching set in each session, then the median of those loads in each period. For example, your usual load for eight split squats might rise from 18 kg to 20 kg.

What’s the difference between a personal record and a best single?

A heaviest set can be one rep or several. A best single is the heaviest weight you actually logged for one rep during the report period. A one-rep set needn’t be an all-out effort, so FITR MAX doesn’t claim it measures your true one-rep maximum.

Which dates do Before and After use?

Before looks back to the first recorded session for that exercise, even if it was before your selected dates. After shows your latest session in the report period. The chart, personal record and training totals use the selected period. The date you joined FITR MAX is separate from the date of your first workout.

What does “Body + load” mean?

It’s your bodyweight plus the external weight recorded for the set. For a lunge, that’s the total supported by both feet; how much falls on each foot depends on your stance. When the report shows a front-foot estimate, it’s an estimate at rest, not a measurement of force during a rep. These estimates don’t increase your lifted volume.

How much weight do my hands support in a push-up?

Your hands and feet share the load. In a force-plate study of trained men with their hands and feet at the same height, the hands supported an average of 62.6% of bodyweight at the top of a push-up. Each 10 kg added in a weight vest increased the load on both hands together by about 7.2 kg. FITR MAX uses those figures for its level-floor estimate. At 41 kg bodyweight, that works out to about 26 kg without extra weight and about 33 kg with 10 kg added. A backpack sits differently from a vest, so its position can change the result. See the force-plate measurements in Table 1.

The load also changes through the rep. In another study, women doing standard push-ups supported about 65% of bodyweight at the top and 70% at the bottom. Foot elevation and hand position change the result. If a log allows either weighted or feet-elevated push-ups, the report labels its estimate as a level-floor reference. A bench press records the mass of the bar and plates. Push-up hand load describes a different movement and cannot predict an individual bench-press result by itself. How do elevated feet and diamond push-ups change the load?

In a study of 23 adults, peak load across both hands averaged 64% of bodyweight during a regular push-up, 70% with feet raised 30.5 cm, and 74% with feet raised 61 cm. For a 41 kg person with no backpack, the three positions correspond to about 26 kg, 29 kg and 30 kg across both hands. These are peak values during an unweighted rep. The study did not test backpacks at those heights. Ebben and colleagues, 2011.

With hands half a shoulder width apart, eight participants supported an average of 65% of their bodyweight on their hands in the starting position. At 41 kg, that gives about 27 kg for this narrow-grip reference. The hands were still spaced apart. A true diamond brings the fingers and thumbs together. Gouvali and Boudolos, Table 1.

Bringing the hands together in a diamond changes which muscles work hardest. Researchers found higher triceps activation with this hand position. Muscle activation alone cannot tell us how many extra kilograms reach the hands. FITR MAX tracks the reps and added weights you log for this variation. What happens when I log a backpack?

A completed push-up set logged at 0 kg counts as bodyweight only, even in a workout that calls for a backpack. Leaving the weight blank keeps it unknown. The report uses the weights recorded in your completed sets and recalculates when you correct an entry. For a level push-up at 41 kg bodyweight, adding 10 kg gives an estimated 33 kg at both hands. During a static hold, the share carried by the hands depends on where the pack sits between the hands and feet. The model assumes a torso load transfers 72% of its mass to the hands, following the vest results. A pack that sits closer to the shoulders can transfer more.

For raised feet, the loaded examples use a separate study that measured pauses at the top and bottom of the rep. Its average bodyweight shares were 72.4% at about 30 cm and 73.9% at 61 cm. For narrow grip the model uses the 65% starting-position reference. Adding the vest-based contribution to either is a model assumption. These studies did not measure loaded elevated or diamond push-ups. For example, 41 kg bodyweight with 10 kg logged gives about 37 kg in both raised-foot examples when rounded to whole kilograms, or 34 kg for the narrow-grip comparison. The exercise label and any recorded backpack weight stay visible. The vest study covered up to 30 kg of added mass. Read the raised-feet static-load study.

What do the object and superhero mean?

The object puts your cumulative lifted weight into perspective. The superhero is a fictional comparison for fun. Neither represents what you could lift in a single attempt.
